# Design process

This is the lightweight pre-production loop shared by the owner and an AI
design partner. It exists to turn uncertainty into evidence without letting
content volume stand in for proof.

## The loop

1. **Orient.** Read `README.md`, `NOW.md`, `MDA.md`, the owning canon and the
   active design test.
2. **Assign the critic.** Follow `CLAUDE.md` §2; this loop schedules that one
   mandatory adversarial reviewer rather than defining another process.
3. **Choose the aesthetic question.** Name one thing the player should feel,
   understand or want. During Core Loop Proof, that is specific voluntary
   anticipation for another descent.
4. **Name the dynamics.** Describe the observable player behaviour that would
   support the aesthetic, plus the failure dynamics that would contradict it.
5. **Inventory the mechanics.** Use `MECHANICS.md` to identify only the rules,
   resources, constraints and content that might cause those dynamics. Ask what
   interesting decision each creates; update the ledger when a mechanic or its
   status changes.
6. **Ask.** Ask at most three high-leverage questions whose answers could change
   the test or decision.
7. **Make a paper pass.** Resolve the first version in markdown, a sketch or a
   table before changing the build.
8. **Attack the paper pass.** Have the critic identify contradictions, drift,
   dominant answers, and missing gameplay reads while changes are still cheap.
9. **Write one design test.** Record the MDA hypothesis, evidence plan and
   pass/revise/stop criteria before testing.
10. **Build the smallest test.** Change only the mechanic needed to distinguish
   the hypothesis from its alternatives.
11. **Self-play.** Remove obvious blockers and tighten the test quickly.
12. **Attack the result.** Give the critic actual native-resolution frames or
    playable behavior. Resolve blockers or record contrary evidence in the
    issue or design test, never as debate in canon.
13. **Cold playtest.** Observe someone who did not help make the test.
14. **Diagnose.** Compare observed dynamics with the target aesthetic. If the
    aesthetic is absent, locate the dynamic that broke before changing a
    mechanic.
15. **Decide.** Accept, revise or cut the claim or mechanic; then update its
    canon owner if the decision changes current truth.

During pre-production, keep one active proof question and one active design
test. Finish or close it before opening another.

## Two kinds of session

A **design session** resolves an uncertain player-experience claim. It changes
the MDA hypothesis, a paper artifact, a design test or canon after evidence.

An **implementation session** executes an already-decided test or
specification. It may reveal a design problem, but it does not silently redesign
the claim. Put the uncertainty back into the active design test.

Outside criticism is adversarial review, not human evidence. It cannot satisfy a
cold-playtest gate.

Every session ends with:

- one updated MDA relationship or confirmed assumption;
- one decision;
- one next test.

## Stage gates

| Gate | What must be true before advancing |
|---|---|
| Concept | The player fantasy, pillars and core loop form a coherent promise. |
| Core-loop proof | Cold players demonstrate the intended loop and voluntarily want another cycle for a specific in-game reason. |
| Vertical-slice proof | A later representative slice proves the production-quality experience and pipeline. |
| Production | The proven slice can be repeated within the scope ceiling without new foundational systems. |
| Ship | A stranger can install, understand and finish the promised prologue. |

The project is at **Core-loop proof**. A promising test does not earn production
content. It earns the next smallest test.
