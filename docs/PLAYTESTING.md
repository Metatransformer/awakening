# Playtesting

Playtesting collects behavior before explanation. The active MDR says what claim
is being tested and what evidence can change the decision.

## During a session

1. State that the player may stop when they choose. Do not imply that a second
   descent is expected.
2. Observe before interviewing. Do not teach unless the session is blocked.
   Record any teaching as a block and note exactly what was said.
3. Record facts and timecodes separately from interpretations. “Opened the bag
   at 18:42” is a fact; “was worried about loss” is an interpretation.
4. Capture brief verbatim quotes when they explain a choice.
5. After play, ask neutral recall and counterfactual questions: “What happened
   when you returned?”, “What would you do next?”, and “What would have made you
   choose differently?”
6. Tag friction as `control`, `comprehension`, `readability`, `decision`, `pace`
   or `desire`. Use more than one tag when needed.

Do not coach toward MDR criteria, defend the game or turn an interview into a
design workshop. Compare behavior with the criteria after the session. Synthesize
only after the full batch, not while one player is still setting the story.

## Two evidence channels

**Designer self-play** is fast and useful for iteration, broken paths and feel.
It is weak evidence for desire: the designer already knows what exists beyond
the test.

**Cold playtests** are gate evidence. The player should not have helped make the
slice and should not be briefed on what the design hopes they will want.

Use [`playtests/PLAYTEST_TEMPLATE.md`](playtests/PLAYTEST_TEMPLATE.md) for raw
session notes. Put batch results and the resulting decision in the active MDR.
